## Hitashi Dubey

**Radiologist (MD) · learning to build the tools I want to use.**

I'm a radiologist with an MD in Radiology (Imaging & Nuclear Medicine) from Kunming Medical University, China.
My research background is clinical: a 173-patient retrospective study in hepatocellular carcinoma, correlating
preoperative MRI features, serum inflammatory markers and immunohistochemical biomarkers against microvascular
invasion grade assigned by pathologists.

I'm now working toward medical imaging AI, and I'm learning the engineering side in public. That is what this
account is for.

### What's here

**`medical-imaging-portfolio`**: three small projects on the theme of trustworthy labels. A reader-agreement
audit (what is a radiology label actually worth?), a transparent liver feature-extraction pipeline, and a
portfolio brief. Built with Python, SimpleITK and scikit-image.

> **Every project here is an illustrative skill demo, not a real finding.** They run on public and synthetic
> data. The numbers they produce describe phantoms and public datasets. They say nothing about any patient,
> and nothing in this account is a research result.

### Where I'm honest about the level

I'm not a machine-learning engineer, and I don't present myself as one. I don't train models. What I do build
is the pipeline around them: Python, self-taught and AI-assisted, end to end. Feature extraction with
SimpleITK and scikit-image, reproducible project structure, QC overlays. That boundary is real and I'd rather
draw it myself than have it found.

What I do bring is the layer underneath the model: deciding what a label actually means, knowing which imaging
findings are reliable enough to score, and building ground truth a pathologist will sign off on. In my
dissertation that meant five years of hospital records, explicit inclusion and exclusion criteria, and imaging
correlated against pathology case by case. In clinical AI that layer is usually the bottleneck, and it's rarely
the part that gets built carefully.
